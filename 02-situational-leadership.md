
# 🎯 Module 2: Situational Leadership (SLII)

In technical organizations, there is no single "correct" leadership style. Managing engineers effectively requires adapting your approach based on an individual’s competence and commitment regarding a **specific task**.

Applying a uniform management style across an entire team leads either to micromanagement (over-supervising an expert) or under-support (abandoning a novice).

---

## 1. The 4 Development Levels (D1 – D4)

Before choosing a leadership style, diagnose the team member's **Development Level** for the given task. Development levels are a combination of **Competence** (knowledge, skills, productivity) and **Commitment** (motivation, confidence):

| Level | Name | Competence | Motivation / Confidence | Behavior Profile |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **D1** | **Enthusiastic Beginner** | Low | High | High energy and eagerness, but lacks technical experience or domain familiarity. |
| **D2** | **Disillusioned Learner** | Low / Moderate | Low | Has gained some experience, realized the task is harder than expected, and lost initial enthusiasm. |
| **D3** | **Capable but Cautious** | Moderate / High | Variable | Possesses strong technical skills, but lacks confidence or self-reliance to execute independently. |
| **D4** | **Self-Reliant Achiever** | High | High | Highly competent, productive, autonomous, and confident in delivering outcomes. |

---

## 2. The 4 Leadership Styles (S1 – S4)

Leadership behavior is defined by two dimensions:
* **Directive Behavior:** Extent to which a leader structures, teaches, supervises, and evaluates work.
* **Supportive Behavior:** Extent to which a leader listens, asks, encourages, facilitates, and explains the "Why".

  HIGH SUPPORT  ▲
                │   [ S3: SUPPORTIVE ]          [ S2: COACHING ]
  SUPPORTIVE    │   (High Support, Low Dir)     (High Support, High Dir)
  BEHAVIOR      │   
                │   [ S4: DELEGATING ]          [ S1: DIRECTIVE ]
                │   (Low Support, Low Dir)      (Low Support, High Dir)
  LOW SUPPORT   └───────────────────────────────────────────────────►
                    LOW DIRECTIVE             HIGH DIRECTIVE
```text

                                DIRECTIVE BEHAVIOR
Style Breakdown & Key Keywords
S1: Directive Style (For D1 - Enthusiastic Beginners)

Keywords: Organize, Structure, Teach, Supervise, Evaluate
Focus: Define the What, How, When, and Where. Provide explicit step-by-step guidance.


S2: Coaching Style (For D2 - Disillusioned Learners)

Keywords: Explain Why, Listen, Ask, Supervise, Encourage
Focus: Maintain high directive structure while providing emotional support and explaining the rationale behind decisions.

S3: Supportive Style (For D3 - Capable but Cautious)

Keywords: Facilitate, Encourage, Ask, Listen, Share
Focus: Shift decision-making to the engineer. Act as a sounding board to build their confidence.

S4: Delegating Style (For D4 - Self-Reliant Achievers)

Keywords: Empower, Trust, Observe, Evaluate
Focus: Grant high autonomy over execution and problem-solving, focusing purely on high-level outcomes.

3. Practical Diagnosis Matrix for Engineering Leaders
When assigning a project, feature, or operational task, use this diagnostic framework:

  ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  1. Identify the Task: Define the specific goal or deliverable.        │
  │  2. Diagnose Development Level: Assess task competence & confidence.  │
  │  3. Select Leadership Style: Match S1, S2, S3, or S4 behavior.          │
  │  4. Agree on an Action Plan: Align on check-ins and autonomy bounds.    │
  └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
