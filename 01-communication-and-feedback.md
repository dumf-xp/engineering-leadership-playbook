# 💬 Communication & Feedback Loops

Effective technical leadership relies on high-clarity communication and short, constructive feedback loops. In fast-moving engineering environments, communication breakdowns lead to rework, misalignment, and operational friction. 

This module outlines actionable frameworks for active listening, feedback delivery, and self-awareness.

---

## 1. Active Listening & Perception Filters

Communication is rarely 100% loss-less. Every message sent passes through the speaker's and listener's personal filters (upbringing, cultural background, unconscious bias, past experiences).

[SPEAKER] ──(Filters)──► [VERBAL / VOCAL / BODY] ──(Filters)──► [LISTENER]
Intended Meaning                                             Perceived Meaning

### The 7-38-55 Rule of Communication
When conveying feelings or attitudes, information is transmitted through three distinct channels:
* **Verbal (Words):** 7% of message impact
* **Tone / Vocal:** 38% of message impact
* **Body Language / Visual:** 55% of message impact

> **Key Operational Takeaway:** Written communication (email, Slack) lacks 93% of the context cues. When alignment breaks down over chat, immediately switch to a quick video call or face-to-face sync.

### Active Listening Techniques
* **Active Silence:** Allow the speaker to finish their thought completely without interjection.
* **Clarify & Probe:** Use open questions (*"How might we...?"*, *"What if...?"*) to understand intent rather than making assumptions.
* **Body Language:** Maintain direct eye contact, open posture, and concentrated focus during 1:1s and incident reviews.

## 2. The Johari Window: Expanding Transparency

The **Johari Window** is a cognitive tool designed to build trust and self-awareness within engineering teams by expanding the "Open Area" through continuous feedback and sharing.

| | **Known by Self** | **Unknown by Self** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Known by Others** | **OPEN AREA**<br>*(Public knowledge, shared context)* | **BLIND SPOT**<br>*(Discovered via **Feedback**)* |
| **Unknown by Others** | **HIDDEN / CLOSED AREA**<br>*(Shared via **Self-Disclosure**)* | **UNKNOWN**<br>*(Undiscovered potential/skills)*|

### Practical Leadership Application
* **Solicit Feedback:** Ask team members for input to shrink your own **Blind Spot**.
* **Practice Transparency:** Share context, strategic rationale, and challenges to reduce the **Hidden Area**.
* **Build Trust:** Expanding the Open Area creates a psychological baseline where engineers feel safe taking calculated risks.

## 3. The 5 Guidelines for Effective Feedback

Feedback is a gift designed to reinforce positive behavior or redirect unproductive habits. Use these 5 core guidelines when delivering feedback:

1. **Specific:** Focus on exact behavior rather than general personality traits.
2. **Timely / Near in Time:** Deliver feedback as close to the event as possible while context is fresh.
3. **Honest & Direct:** Speak directly with authentic intent; avoid passive-aggressive messaging.
4. **Focused on Behavior & Impact:** Describe *Specific Behavior* $\rightarrow$ *Consequences* $\rightarrow$ *Personal/Team Impact*.
5. **Actionable / Redirecting:** Clearly outline what good behavior looks like or agree on concrete next steps.

---

## 4. The Feedback Staircase

When receiving feedback, human reactions typically follow a psychological "staircase". Lower steps represent defensive barriers, while higher steps represent growth.

[ 5. CHANGE ]       ▲  Actionable growth & behavior shift
[ 4. UNDERSTAND ]   │  Asking clarifying questions
[ 3. EXPLAIN ]      │  Adding context without being defensive
--------------------┼-----------------------------------------
[ 2. DEFEND / ARGUE]│  Pushing back or making excuses
[ 1. DENIAL ]       │  Rejecting the feedback outright


### Goal for Leaders
Help engineers climb past *Denial* and *Defend/Argue* toward **Understanding** and **Change**. If emotions are high during a feedback session, take a pause (*Time Out OK!*) before proceeding.
